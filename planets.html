Planets

MERCURY

Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun and the smallest in the Solar System. It has a rocky surface covered in craters and almost no atmosphere. Because of this, temperatures change extremely between day and night. A full orbit around the Sun takes 88 Earth days.

VENUS

Venus is similar in size to Earth but has a very thick atmosphere made mostly of carbon dioxide. This traps heat, making it the hottest planet in the Solar System. Its surface is rocky with volcanic features, and it rotates in the opposite direction to most planets.

EARTH

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only known planet that supports life. It has liquid water on its surface and a nitrogen-oxygen atmosphere. Its climate and oceans support a wide variety of ecosystems.

MARS

Mars is a cold, desert-like planet often called the Red Planet due to iron oxide on its surface. It has mountains, valleys, and polar ice caps. Evidence suggests it once had flowing water and possibly conditions suitable for life.

JUPITER

Jupiter is the largest planet in the Solar System and is made mostly of hydrogen and helium. It has a strong magnetic field and a famous storm called the Great Red Spot. It also has many moons, including large ones like Ganymede.

SATURN

Saturn is a gas giant known for its bright and extensive ring system made of ice and rock particles. It is less dense than water and has many moons, including Titan, which has a thick atmosphere.

URANUS

.Uranus is an ice giant with a blue-green color caused by methane in its atmosphere. It rotates on its side, making its seasons extreme. It has a cold atmosphere and faint rings.

NEPTUNE

Neptune is the farthest planet from the Sun and an ice giant with strong winds and storms. It appears deep blue due to methane gas. It has a dynamic atmosphere and several moons, including Triton.
